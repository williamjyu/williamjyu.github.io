**Properties of the 2x2x2 Rubik's Cube in Graph Theory**

With Manolo Dal Bo · Brown University · April 2026

We model the 2x2x2 Rubik's Cube as a group and study its Cayley graph:

- **Group notation.** Each state of the cube is a permutation of its 24 stickers, so the cube group is the subgroup of S<sub>24</sub> generated by the quarter turns R, R′, U, U′, F, F′.
- **Cayley graph.** Vertices are cube states; two states are adjacent when a single quarter turn takes one to the other.
- **Bipartite structure.** Every generator is an odd permutation, so in the quarter-turn metric the graph splits into even and odd states, with the solved state on the even side.
- **Hamiltonian laceability.** If the graph is laceable, then from any odd scramble there is a path of length 3,674,159 that visits every state exactly once before solving the cube.
- **Vertex transitivity.** Brute force over 3+ million states is infeasible, but every Cayley graph is vertex-transitive. That lets us fix one end of the path at the solved state, so it suffices to find a Hamiltonian path from each odd state to the identity.

The talk also covered the cube's history on college campuses, from MIT's CubeLovers list (1980) to the 2010 proof of God's Number.
